## [2026-11-07]

### Fixed
- **False all-time Best DX (17 038 km)** — a transmission of 16 July 2024 had its farthest distance
  from SM4HVP, whose stale locator (JP70od) is 538 km away. The Analysis page already left such
  reports out; now `suspect_dx()` (the distances the implausible reporters of `find_outliers()`
  gave beyond what their grid allows, from the new `replay_db.get_pair_distances()`, cached an hour
  and refreshed by the background warm-up) is left out of `db.get_stats_by_date()`,
  `get_all_time_stats()` and `get_daily_summary()` (`exclude_dx`, via `db._max_dx()`), and such a
  distance is given as 0 (unknown) in `/api/positions`, `/api/latest` and the WPRS days, trips and
  tracks. The databases are not changed. The record is now 15 619 km; only that one transmission
  is affected in the station's data.
- **Odd reporter country names** — `callsign_to_country()` gave the first two characters of an
  unknown prefix ("14", "27", "HF", "EU") and coarse regions for SWL names and mismatched prefixes
  ("W. Europe", "UK/Ireland", and "N. Africa" for Greek stations). It now uses the new
  `core/grid_country.py` (every 4-character Maidenhead square and its countries, generated by
  `tools/make_grid_country.py` from the Natural Earth 1:50m borders, with each 6-character
  subsquare of the 1 706 border squares): the prefix's country is kept when it is at the
  locator, otherwise the locator's country is used; a region only at sea. New prefixes: EU, EV
  (Belarus), HF (Poland), YT (Serbia), 5P, 5Q (Denmark), 9H (Malta), JW (Svalbard), JX (Jan Mayen),
  BD, BZ (China), BW, BX (Taiwan), AH, AJ (USA), UU–UW (Ukraine), UO (Kazakhstan), DE, DI (Germany),
  SB–SI (Sweden). Labels are worked out when read, so Replay of earlier days shows them too.
- **History / Statistics showed the wrong day** — a slower answer for an earlier date could
  replace the date chosen last (`views.js` `loadHistory()`, `loadStats()`, the phone's History);
  older answers are now ignored.
- **Papers: version line** — the title line of the propagation study and the WPRS paper said
  version 1.1 while the page header and the version history said 1.2; both now say 1.2 (rebuilt,
  otherwise unchanged).
- **Unused imports** — `statistics` in `doc/src/analyze.py`, `wspr_logger` in
  `doc/src/analyze_wprs.py`.
- **README** — the Theme button row stood below the sidebar table (shown as plain text); it is in
  the table now, with rows for *What's new* and *Phone app*; the clone address is
  `https://github.com/oh2gax/wspr_logger.git`.

### Changed
- **Analysis: 7 d and 14 d, opening at 14 days** — `_ANALYSIS_DAYS` is now 7, 14, 30, 90, 180, 365
  (and 0 = all); `_analysis_body.html` and the phone menu have *7 d* and *14 d*, and the page opens
  at 14 days (`analysis.js` `_anDays`). Until a period is chosen by hand, a period without reports
  (the transmitter is off, usually November–March) steps on to the next longer one, and the dates
  line says why (*no reports in the last 14 days (transmitter off) — showing 30 days*); a period
  chosen by hand that has no reports says so instead of *No replay data stored yet*.
- **Analysis: computed ahead, and a loading message** — `_warm_analysis()` computes the periods
  opened without a choice — 14 days (the page) and 90 days (the phone's Now view), plus the longer
  periods the page would step to when 14 days are empty — of `/api/analysis` and
  `/api/analysis/more`, and the DX outlook model, in a background thread after the start-up fetch
  and after every poll (about 3–7 s of CPU every 10 minutes). Those entries are kept 20 minutes
  instead of 10, so a visitor always
  gets the ready one (before: 4–8 s on the first opening in each 10 minutes, the usual case on a
  quiet site). The caches go through `_cached()`, which also lets concurrent requests for the same
  key wait for one computation instead of each running it; the route bodies moved into
  `_build_analysis()` / `_build_more()` (output byte-identical, checked for 90 d, 30 d and all).
  `analysis.js`: while waiting, the first opening shows a spinner and a message with the seconds
  waited; a period change keeps the cards dimmed with *Loading …* beside the period buttons; an
  older, slower answer no longer replaces the period chosen last; a failed load leaves the old cards.
- **About: Availability** — a new row in the About window (`static/js/info.js`) and the phone's
  About sheet (`templates/app.html`): data comes only while the car's WSPR transmitter is running
  (30 000 mAh batteries, so sometimes off); typically on 24/7 in the summer season (March–November)
  and off in the winter season.
- **`/api/reporter_list`** — each reporter also carries its `country` (used by the phone's
  Reporters search and country bars).

- **WPRS: 150 km and 200 km filters** — the *Days with movement or distance from home of at least*
  list has 150 km and 200 km steps after 100 km, for picking out the long trips only.
- **User guide** — `doc/WSPR_Logger_User_Guide.pdf` (25 pages, version 1.0), in the style of the
  Mode-S Wind user guides: WSPR in a nutshell and how the logger collects its data, the page and the
  menu, the live map and every layer, the History Player, the History and Statistics pages, the
  Propagation Analysis page card by card (what each chart shows, how to read it, what this
  station's data says, what to watch out for), the WPRS page, questions and where to look,
  limitations and a quick reference. All figures are screenshots of the page with the station's
  own data. Source `doc/src/user_guide.tex`, screenshots in `doc/src/guide_img/`. The Guides list
  is now in a fixed order: the user guide, the WPRS two-page summary, the WPRS paper and the
  propagation study.
- **Analysis: seven new cards** — computed by the new module `core/analysis_more.py` and the
  endpoint `/api/analysis/more` (cached 10 minutes; the whole-history inputs for an hour), drawn by
  the new script `static/js/analysis-more.js`. They load separately from the rest of the page.
  - *Where you are heard* — polar chart of the stations by direction from the home square
    (16 sectors) and distance (rings at 1000, 2000, 3000, 6000 and 10 000 km), stations or reports,
    with hover details and a summary of the busiest directions.
  - *Distance distribution* — stations per 500 km (logarithmic) and the share of reports.
  - *Band opening and closing* — the first and last transmission of each UTC day heard by 3 or
    more stations, with sunrise and sunset at the home square.
  - *Season against season* — the same months of each year side by side: median daytime
    (08–17 UTC) stations per snapshot or the share of snapshots with DX beyond 6000 km.
  - *Stations per month* — different stations per month and stations never heard before.
  - *Signal strength* — SNR median and 25–75 % range by UTC hour and by 1000 km of distance.
  - *Unusual days* — the eight best and worst days of the period, daytime stations against what is
    typical for that month of the year and hour, with ▶ links into the History Player.
  - *Most loyal reporters* — the 15 stations heard in the largest share of snapshots, with
    distance, average and best SNR and the first day heard.
  New queries `replay_db.get_station_stats()`, `get_month_stations()`, `get_snr_table()`.
- **Day / Night in the History Player** — the Day / Night layer drew the terminator of the
  present moment even while an old snapshot was replayed. It now follows the replayed time
  (`dayNightTime()`), redraws with every frame, and returns to the present on *Live ✕*.
- **Analysis: 6 m and 1 yr periods** — the period buttons of the Propagation Analysis page are
  now 30 d, 90 d, 6 m, 1 yr and All. `/api/analysis` rounds `days` up to 30, 90, 180 or 365
  (was 30, 90, 180 or 400), so the *1 yr* button is exactly one year; the *Farthest reporters*
  title names the period ("last 6 months", "last year").
- **Analysis: Ionosonde view over longer periods** — the *Ionosonde day view* became the
  *Ionosonde view* with its own buttons: *Day* (as before, every snapshot of one day), *30 d*,
  *90 d*, *6 m*, *1 yr*, *All* and *Custom* (first and last day). Longer periods are averaged
  over buckets of 1 h, 3 h, 6 h, 12 h, 1 day or 2 days so that there are at most 500 bars; empty
  buckets stay empty, so gaps such as the winters show. New endpoint
  `/api/iono?from=&to=` / `?days=` (cached 10 minutes) and `replay_db.get_iono_series()`,
  `replay_db.get_first_timestamp()`. Polls that heard no station now count as 0 stations
  (before, the day view skipped them), so the MUF line no longer breaks off at night.
- **WPRS two-page summary under Guides** — `doc/WPRS_Two_Page_Summary.pdf`: the idea, how
  a fix travels, the station, a table of the key measurements, the position map and the
  availability figure, where WPRS fits, limitations and operating tips. Built from the new
  `doc/src/wprs_brief.tex`, which uses the same macros (`out/numbers.tex`,
  `out/wprs_numbers.tex`) and figures as the full paper, so it stays in step when the
  analysis is re-run. Listed in `doc/guides.json` right after the full paper.

### Fixed
- **Mini-stats title said "Last 1h" while showing today** — the page starts on the *Today* tab
  (the default), but the card title said *Last 1h* until a tab was clicked. The README also called
  the 1-hour view the default.
- **History Player bar on four rows** — a general rule that makes date fields and select boxes
  full width also stretched the date and speed fields of the History Player bar, which then
  wrapped onto four rows (166 px high instead of 49 px). In the History Player and drive replay
  bars those fields now take their natural width.
- **Code audit, 7 November** — findings of a review of the whole program, each tested with the
  real databases:
  - *Invalid locators became positions* — `maidenhead_to_latlon()` converted anything six
    characters long, so a garbled locator such as `ZZ99zz` gave latitude 170°. It now accepts only
    valid locators (A–R, digits, A–X) and returns nothing otherwise; the live poll and
    `tools/backfill.py` skip such spots.
  - *`/api/iono` crashed on extreme dates* (`to=9999-12-31` gave a 500) and a range of centuries
    produced 10,000+ empty points. Bad dates are a 400, and the range is limited to the data
    (first snapshot to today).
  - *A stale MUF was shown as the present one* — `/api/solar` (J-MUF in the Solar panel) and
    ⚡ HIGH MUF used the newest stored reading however old (16 hours in the test). A reading older
    than 3 hours is now shown as —, the same rule the Analysis page already used
    (`_latest_muf()`, shared by `/api/latest`, `/api/solar` and `/api/analysis`).
  - *"& DX" and ⚡ HIGH MUF depended on other switches* — the Propagation card counted reporters
    beyond 6000 km from the reporter list, which was only fetched with the Reporter List, Plot or
    Histogram on, and took the MUF from the Solar panel. `/api/latest` now carries `dx6` and `muf`,
    so both work with any layers.
  - *The History Player skipped every poll that heard nobody* — 51 of 96 frames on 6 November; the
    slider jumped over the night and the Solar panel had no night values. Such polls are now frames
    with an empty reporter list (`replay_db.get_day_frames()`).
  - *Reporter Plot put 4-character locators in the corner of their square* — the subsquare offset
    was added to a square without a subsquare; they are now placed at the centre of the 2° × 1° square.
  - *One wspr.live query fewer per poll* — the reporter countries were fetched with a separate
    query for exactly the same stations as the reporter list; they are now worked out from the list
    (`countries_of()` replaces `fetch_reporter_countries()`).
  - The beacon's locator in map popups and WPRS tooltips goes through `escHtml()` like other
    text from wspr.live.
- **Analysis left out 42 ordinary stations (about 19 % of the reports)** — on 7 May 2025
  (10:08–15:58 UTC, a season rebuilt from wspr.live) wspr.live delivered a distance of 0 for 98
  reports. The outlier check took 0 km as a reported distance, found it contradicting the
  stations' own locators and excluded those 42 European stations (DL7KST, G4FUI, HB9VQQ, …) from
  the whole history: 581,947 of 3.09 million reports. `replay_db.get_reporter_pairs()` now leaves
  distances of 0 out of the check, so only the three real contradictions remain excluded
  (G8LZI/2, SM4HVP, SV1CDN, the same three as in the papers, whose `analyze.py` was not
  affected). Effect on the page, whole history: average stations heard per snapshot 46.6 → 56.3,
  snapshots 53,914 → 54,868; last 90 days 57.8 → 65.2.
- **Reporter Countries window too wide after it was moved** — a moved window is restored with the
  width saved together with its position, and older versions of the page saved a wider one
  (before the right-hand column got its fixed width). A moved Reporter Countries window now always
  has the width of the column, 238 px, the same as the Current Position and Propagation cards.

## [2026-11-06]

### Added
- **What's new under the title, in plain prose** — *What's new* is now a link with the
  version label under the *WSPR Logger* title instead of a menu item. The window reads
  `doc/whats_new.md` (new, hand-written, Mode-S Wind style: version heading, headline,
  bullets with a bold lead) through `core/changelog.py` instead of listing Added / Changed /
  Fixed entries from this file.
- **Last fix age in the Locator area** — the left pane shows *Last fix N min ago* below
  *Last Spot (UTC)*, which now has minute resolution (`2026-11-06 16:42`).
- **WPRS page — the car's movements** — new sidebar page **WPRS** (Whisper Position
  Reporting System). Every transmission carries the locator the transmitter computed from
  its GPS fix, so the logged spots are a position track. A *drive detector* lists the days
  on which the car moved: per day the squares used, the largest distance between two squares
  (moved), the path length and the farthest distance from the home square (the most
  frequent locator), so days parked far from home are caught too. A filter (any change of
  square, 5, 10 — the default —, 25 or 100 km) and a 1 / 2 / 3 / 5-day span select what is
  shown. The map draws the visited Maidenhead squares (shaded by number of fixes), the
  track line with a dot per fix (hover for time, locator, reporters and best DX), start,
  end and home. A *drive replay* bar plays the fixes in order (1–16 fixes per second, slider,
  to start) with the trail so far, and **History Player** opens the shown moment in the
  History Player on the live map. New module `wprs.py`, endpoints `/api/wprs/days` and
  `/api/wprs/track?from=&to=` (up to 7 days), `db.get_all_spots()`.
- **Guides — PDF documents on the page** — new sidebar entry **Guides** opens a window
  that lists the PDF files of the new `doc/` folder with *Open* and *Download* links.
  Titles, descriptions and dates come from `doc/guides.json` (a PDF without an entry is
  listed under its file name). New module `guides.py`, endpoints `/api/guides` and
  `/doc/<file>` (only plain `*.pdf` names inside `doc/` are served). *What's new* and
  *User guides* are two tabs of one window, as on the Mode-S Wind page: both sidebar
  entries open it, on the tab of the entry.
- **Movable map panels** — the History / reporter list, the histogram and the country
  overlay can be dragged by their title bar anywhere on the map (kept inside the map; the
  position is remembered, double-click the title to reset). The date/time card, the
  Propagation overlay and the MUF D=3000 graph stay where they were.
- **Fix age on the live map** — a label beside the car marker and a line in the Current
  Position card say how old the latest fix is (for example "last fix 38 min ago"), so a stale
  position is not mistaken for the present one. The new **Last Fix Label** switch in Map Layers
  hides the label on the map (remembered; the line in the card stays).
- **Share links** — the address of the page follows the view: a day, a moment in the
  History Player or a WPRS day and fix can be copied and opened by someone else
  (`#...` links; WPRS has a *Copy link* button).
- **WPRS trip summary cards** — consecutive days away from home (gap of at most 36 h, at
  least 25 km from home) are grouped into trips with dates, duration, squares, path length
  and farthest point. A *Days / Trips* switch in the list; selecting a trip draws it on the
  map and replays it. `wprs.build_trips()`, `trips` in `/api/wprs/days`.
- **Analysis: outlier filter** — a reporter whose distance contradicts its own locator by
  more than 500 km (a mistyped or stale grid) is dropped from the analysis and the records,
  and listed in a collapsible *Excluded reports* card. `excluded` in `/api/analysis`.
- **Analysis: grey-line card** — whether long-distance reports cluster around the sunrise
  and sunset at the far end or at home, with the hour of the day held fixed, compared with
  the share expected by chance (z score). New modules `sun.py` and `greyline.py`;
  `greyline` in `/api/analysis`.
- **Analysis: DX outlook card** — for the next 12 hours the chance of a report beyond
  6000 km, from the hour-of-day history with shrinkage towards the average, adjusted for the
  present MUF and K only if that adjustment beat the hour of the day alone in a
  chronological back-test (otherwise the card says so). New module `outlook.py`;
  `outlook`, `outlook_model` in `/api/analysis`. Always fitted on the whole history.
- **History backfill from wspr.live** — `tools/backfill.py` fills the replay database with
  the snapshots of earlier seasons by reconstructing, from the spots wspr.live holds for
  the callsign, what the :08 poll would have listed. Run it on the server:
  `venv/bin/python tools/backfill.py --test 2025-08-15`, then for example
  `venv/bin/python tools/backfill.py --from 2024-07-01`. Existing snapshots are
  never overwritten; solar and MUF data are not backfilled. `--compare DATE` checks rebuilt snapshots against the live ones of the same days. See README.
- **Two scientific PDF notes in `doc/`** — *Twenty-metre WSPR propagation study*
  (three seasons, 2024–2026, 495 days with data, the first two rebuilt from wspr.live: diurnal and seasonal patterns, season comparison, geography, DX windows,
  space-weather effects, a storm case study) and *WPRS – Whisper Position Reporting
  System* (position beaconing over HF with the 200 mW GPS transmitter and the
  car-roof antenna: locator resolution, fix availability, tracks, power reach, battery
  budget). Every figure, table and number is generated from the databases by
  `doc/src/analyze.py` and `doc/src/analyze_wprs.py`; the LaTeX sources are in `doc/src/`.
- **Propagation Analysis page** — new sidebar page **Analysis**, computed from the
  replay database (no wspr.live queries, cached 10 minutes). A *band-open heatmap*
  (UTC hour by date, coloured by average reporters or farthest distance, with
  hover details), a *typical day* chart (median and 25–75 % range per UTC hour with
  the latest day on top), reporters versus Juliusruh *MUF*, *K-index* and *SFI*, and
  a table of *geomagnetic storm days* (K ≥ 5). Period buttons 30 d / 90 d / All.
  The bin charts show an index (100 = typical number of reporters for that UTC
  hour) so the day/night rhythm does not masquerade as a space-weather effect;
  solar readings of 0 (feed glitches) are ignored. New module `analysis.py`,
  endpoint `/api/analysis?days=&band=`, `replay_db.get_snapshot_rows()`.
- **Analysis: "Right now" comparison** — the live number of stations heard is shown
  next to what is usual for this UTC hour (median and 25–75 % range) and, when
  there is enough history, for a similar Juliusruh MUF (same 2 MHz bin), with a
  verdict: better than usual / typical / below usual. A MUF reading older than
  3 hours is ignored, and no verdict is given while wspr.live is down.
- **Analysis: DX windows** — share of snapshots with a reporter beyond 6000 km and
  the average farthest reporter by UTC hour, plus a month-by-month chart of average
  stations heard and DX share (tooltips show days, snapshots and average SFI).
- **Analysis: farthest reporters and replay links** — table of the ten farthest
  stations of the period (one row per station, with country and grid), and a ▶ button
  on every row of it and of the storm-day table that opens that exact moment in the
  History Player. `replay_db.get_top_distances()`; the player now accepts a start
  moment (`openReplayAt()`).
- **Analysis: Ionosonde day view** — pick any day and see Juliusruh MUF (and foF2,
  for days where it was collected) as lines over the stations heard through that
  day; foF2 history builds up from the day the new foF2 source went live.
- **History player on the live map** — new sidebar switch **History Player** opens
  a player bar at the bottom of the map: play/pause, time slider, date picker with
  previous/next day that has data, 1/2/4/8/16 frames per second, and **Live ✕** to
  return to live data. One frame per stored poll snapshot (~every 10 minutes).
  While replaying, the normal layer switches decide what is shown: Reporter List,
  SNR / Dist Histogram, Reporter Plot (great-circle arcs from the beacon position
  logged at that time), Reporter Countries, and the Solar Conditions panel
  (SFI, K, A, X-ray, Bz, wind, aurora, proton flux, foF2, MUF). The Propagation
  overlay uses the spot logged at that time, or "No propagation" if none was
  logged within 25 minutes. Live refreshes are paused during replay and the page
  repaints from live data on exit. New endpoints `/api/replay/dates` and
  `/api/replay/day?date=YYYY-MM-DD` (a whole day in one ~300 KB response);
  `replay_db.get_day_frames()`; solar panel rendering split into `renderSolar()`.
- **Map Layer toggles are remembered per browser** — the sidebar switches and their
  sub-settings (MUF period, histogram SNR/Dist tab, reporter-list sort, 1h/Today tab)
  are saved to `localStorage` (key `wspr_layers`) on every change and restored on
  page load through the normal toggle functions. Missing or corrupt storage falls
  back to the defaults.
- **What's new and About** — two new entries in the sidebar. *What's new* lists the
  changes of the last 10 days (read from this CHANGELOG by `changelog.py`, endpoint
  `/api/whatsnew`) and shows the version as `v.DDMMYYYY` (date of the newest
  entry), with a NEW marker until it has been opened. *About* shows the station:
  operator, transmitter (200 mW), antenna, home locator and a link to the public
  GitHub repository.
- **Statistics page redesign** — day navigation with previous/next arrows and a
  weekday label, an *average reporters* card, a *Longest DX* card that shows the
  percentage of the all-time record (or a trophy on a record day), a *Through the
  day* chart (reporters per transmission and farthest distance), a *Best DX per
  day* chart (30 d / 90 d / All; click a bar to open that day), all-time records
  with the date each was set, and a clickable list of logged dates. New endpoint
  `/api/daily_summary`.
- **`update.sh` — one-command server update** — pulls the latest version from
  GitHub on a git-clone installation (such as the AWS server), restarts the
  systemd service, and verifies that the web page answers with HTTP 200 again.
  It shows the new commits and changed files first and asks for confirmation
  (`-y` skips the question). `bash update.sh --rollback` returns to the version
  that ran before the last update.
- **Safety checks in `update.sh`** — only fast-forwards (`git merge --ff-only`)
  and never runs `git reset`, `stash`, or `checkout`, so server-local files
  (`config.ini` with `host = 127.0.0.1`, `api_keys.py`, the databases) are never
  overwritten; if GitHub changes a file that is also modified locally, the script
  stops and explains what to do. Changed `.py` files and templates are
  syntax-checked straight from git before anything is touched, so a broken or
  truncated push cannot take the running service down. Both SQLite databases are
  copied to `<install folder>_backups/` with SQLite's online backup before every
  update (the 7 newest copies are kept), and `pip install -r requirements.txt`
  runs only when `requirements.txt` changed.
- **README: Updating on a Server** — a step-by-step recipe for converting a folder
  that was copied rather than cloned into a git checkout without touching
  `config.ini`, `api_keys.py`, the databases or `venv/`; `update.sh` added to the
  project structure.

## [2026-05-23]

### Added
- **wspr.live health check** — a dedicated `check_wsprlive_health()` function runs
  `SELECT count() AS cnt FROM wspr.rx WHERE time > subtractMinutes(now(), 5)` once
  per poll cycle (and immediately on startup). Because there are always thousands of
  WSPR spots globally every few minutes, a count of zero reliably indicates that the
  ClickHouse database is broken or lagging, even when the HTTP connection itself
  succeeds. The `_wspr_live_down` flag is set `True` on either a network failure
  (exception) or an empty count, and cleared only when the health check confirms live
  data is present.
- **"DB Timeout!" propagation indicator** — when `_wspr_live_down` is `True` the
  Propagation overlay shows **DB Timeout!** in amber with a "wspr.live unreachable"
  subtitle, distinguishing a data source outage from "No propagation or TX Fail" and
  making it immediately clear the silence is external rather than a transmission
  problem. The indicator clears automatically on the next successful health check.
- `/api/latest` response now includes a `wspr_down` boolean field so the frontend
  can react to backend connectivity state without any extra API calls.
